# Equifax Data Breach (2017) - Research Notes
# Background:
- Equifax is one of the three major credit reporting agencies in the United States
- They collect and maintain sensitive financial and personal information used by lenders and other organizations to access creditworthiness

- # Investigation / Findings
- In 2017, Equifax experiences a major cybersecurity breach that compromised personal information of approximately 147 million individuals.
- The breach began in May 2017, as was officially discovered in July, before being publicly disclosed in September. Investigators determined that the attackers exploited a known vulnerability in Apache Struts a framework used for Java web applications
- A security patch had previously been released in March the same year, Equifax's internal procedures failed to ensure it was applied to the affected system.
- Investigations also identified weaknesses in a network monitoring that delayed the detection of suspicious activity.

- # Consequences
- Approximately 147 million individuals had their personal information compromised
- The incident created significant privacy and security concerns because said information (Social Security numbers, Names, etc.) are very difficult to replace and are a breach of confidentiality
- Equifax subsequently faced regulatory investigations, lawsuits and a major settlement with federal and state authorities.

- # Connections to Software Development
- The Equifax Data Breach Incident demonstrates that cybersecurity responsibilities extends far beyond the initial development of software. Organizations should regularly maintain their applications, monitor third-party frameworks and always verify that security updates have been successfully implemented
